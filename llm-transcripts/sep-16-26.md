# You're right about rating, and here is exactly what happened

`★ Insight
─────────────────────────────────────`
**Vanilla:** an item's text said "+1% critical strike chance." The game stored a
percentage and added it straight into the crit roll. Level cap 60, gear tuned so
a full best set landed somewhere sane.

**Burning Crusade:** ten more levels, and a fresh tier of loot every patch. Flat
percentages left two bad choices — put small percentages on new gear so an
upgrade barely moves the number and feels like nothing, or put big ones on and
watch players cross 100% and break the roll.

**The fix:** change the currency. Gear stopped giving percent and gave **crit
rating**, a plain unbounded integer. The game divides that integer by a number
that grows with your character level. Roughly 14 rating per 1% at level 60,
about 22 at 70, about 46 at 80. The same 100-rating item is worth around 7% at
60 and around 2% at 80. Nobody edits the item. It decays on its own as you
climb.

**The rule in one line:** never let a player-facing number live in the same
units as a probability. Put a divisor in between, and let the divisor grow with
progression.
`─────────────────────────────────────────────────`

Now the part that should feel good.

**You already invented rating.** "One extra point per 100 strength" *is* a
divisor. Strength is the rating, 100 is the divisor, percentage points come out
the far end. You built the same machine Blizzard built, without the name for it.

The one piece you're missing is that theirs **grows**. Yours is nailed to 100.

Whether that matters comes down to a single number, and it's yours to give:
**what is the biggest strength a hero will ever have?** Your max level is 20,

# Imagination is going to do something you might not expect

In this game imagination isn't stored anywhere. It is **computed from
regression** every time it's read. And regression swings hard inside a single
fight — a monster lands a hit, she is suddenly full of imagination, and it
drains back toward zero over the next minute.

So "imagination softens the drop" means the softening changes second to second.

A hero who levels a stat **while she is little** keeps most of her ladder. The
same hero, grown up again ten seconds later, loses half of it.

Being little is when the lesson sticks. That is lovely, and it costs you nothing
— it falls straight out of what you already built. It also hands the player a
real move: get a hero small on purpose before a hard fight, because that is when
growth stays.

One guard rail. If imagination can soften the drop all the way to nothing,
ladder one only ever climbs. It reaches 100% and parks there, and that hero
gains a point on **every** roll, forever. So the softening needs a floor.
Halfway down at worst, a fifth of the way down at best. Never zero.

# Two of your answers are secretly one answer

You said monsters have to out-scale heroes forever.

You said stats should be unbounded, and maybe the dice have to go.

**Those are the same decision.** Here is why.

A hundred-sided die rolled under your stat has a top. At 100 strength you never
fail. Not "almost never." Never. And the moment a hero gets there, **no amount
of monster scaling can ever touch her again.** The monster can have a thousand
of anything; her hold check still passes, every single time, forever.

The race you want ends the instant one hero maxes one stat.

So you can't keep both. You already knew that. You just arrived at it from the
other side.

## What goes in the die's place

A **contest**. You stop rolling against a fixed hundred and start rolling your
number against the number that's fighting you.

One line:

> chance to pass = your stat ÷ (your stat + the thing resisting)

Walk it:

| your strength | resisting it | you pass |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 30 | 50% |
| 60 | 30 | 67% |
| 300 | 30 | 91% |
| 300 | 300 | 50% |
| 3000 | 3000 | 50% |

Three things fall out, and all three are things you asked me for:

1. **No ceiling, no floor.** It never reaches 100% and never reaches 0%. There
   is always a chance and always a risk.
2. **Only the ratio matters.** Multiply both sides by ten and nothing changes. A
   hero at 30 against a monster at 30 is the *same fight* as a hero at 3000
   against a monster at 3000.
3. **That is your race, exactly.** Floors get harder forever, heroes get
   stronger forever, and what decides a run is who grew faster. No number ever
   makes a hero safe.

`★ Insight
─────────────────────────────────────`
This shape has a name — it's the same maths behind chess Elo ratings and
behind most "opposed roll" systems. The useful property is that it turns an
**absolute** number into a **relative** one. A die under a stat asks "how good
are you?" A contest asks "how much better are you than this?" Only the second
question has an answer that stays meaningful when both sides have been growing
for six floors.

Your whole game is the second question. The hold check is a hero's strength
against her own fullness. Combat is her against a monster. Puzzles are her
intellect against the puzzle. You never actually needed the first question.

# Your growth is a square, not a snowball

You said you want a bit of exponential scaling. I went and worked out what your
two ladders actually produce, and I have good news: **it's gentler than you
think.**

Here is the reason. Ladder one is a percentage, and a percentage tops out at
100. So the faster it climbs, the sooner it hits, and the less the extra
climbing speed buys you. Doubling how fast the ladder climbs does *not* double
how fast the stat grows. It multiplies it by about 1.4 — the square root of
two.

Points of stat gained per roll, once the ladder has settled into its rhythm:

| strength | ladder climbs by | stat points per roll |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | about 0.15 |
| 100 | 2 | about 0.20 |
| 500 | 6 | about 0.35 |
| 1000 | 11 | about 0.47 |

Read the two ends. Going from 0 strength to 1000 strength — the whole range
you said you want — only **triples** how fast you grow. It does not multiply
it by a thousand.

What that means over a run: **a stat grows like time squared.** Twice as long
adventuring, four times the stat. Not eight times, not a thousand times. A
square.

So your monsters don't have to beat a snowball. They have to beat a square. Give
a floor's monsters a difficulty that grows like time *cubed*, and the heroes
lose eventually no matter what, and the only question is how many floors they
got through first. That is a much kinder number to tune than racing a real
exponential.

proving ground to confirm them, not me.)

## 1. The sliders already do this

Why I like this most:

- **Nothing is contrived.** No fake event, no pretending being hit is a

- **There is no opaque number.** You said you hate those. The scaling factor

- **Your class and your stats can never disagree.** The same slider decides both

The other way to divide. A hero adventures, and the game counts what she

# The two you asked me to pin down

## Imagination's floor

You said linear on regression, and you're right that it has to be. Here's the
exact number and why.

The drop is **halfway back down** at zero imagination. At full imagination it
softens. The only value that actually breaks is **exactly zero drop** — then
the ladder only ever climbs, pins at 100%, and that hero gains a point on every
roll forever.

Any drop at all is safe. I checked what a 10% drop does: the ladder settles at
about 32% instead of about 15%, and the hero grows two to three times faster.
Fast, not runaway.

So: **halfway down at zero imagination, a tenth of the way down at full
imagination, straight line between them.** One entry in the balance table, and
the proving ground moves it.

## Regression is a narrow window — here's the sharper version of your warning

You said don't let regression effects scale exponentially, because 0-to-100 is
narrow. You're right, and there's a crisper rule underneath it:

> **Anything computed from regression must multiply. It must never add.**

A multiplier keeps its worth forever. Imagination tripling your magical damage
is worth 3× at 30 intellect and worth 3× at 3000 intellect. It never goes
stale.

An addition drowns. Imagination *adding* 50 to your magical damage is huge at 30
intellect and invisible at 3000.

That one rule protects every bounded number in the game — regression,
imagination, confidence, pride — from being eaten by the unbounded ones. It's
worth writing into the vocabulary document as a law.

## And your "under a thousand"

You want unbounded *and* under a thousand. Those don't fight, they just need
naming separately:

- **Unbounded** means no rule stops the number. No cap, no clamp, nothing that
  says "you may not exceed."
- **Under a thousand** means in practice a run ends before a hero gets there.

You already said what ends a run: the floors run out, because there are more
ghosts than floors the guild can clear.

So the number you tune isn't a cap. It's **how steeply the floors scale**. Tune
it so a guild that goes as deep as anyone ever goes comes home with heroes in
the high hundreds. Nobody caps anything, and nobody sees four digits.

## There's one formula, and it covers all of them

Let the counter climb by **c** points each time the thing doesn't happen. Let it
fall by the fraction **f** of its height when it does.

> **resting height = 10 × √(c ÷ f)**

The important bit: **both knobs are under a square root.** Double how fast it
climbs, get about one and a half times the rate. Halve the fall, same. **Nobody
can break one of these by turning a dial too far.** That's why they're safe to

## The whole digestion dataflow, end to end

Check this against what you remember:

**Two ways in.** Eating puts a food unit in the tummy — a kind, and a count of
increments left. Drinking adds to the tummy's fluid pool.

**Every food tickle, solids:** every unit in every tummy in the world steps
forward one increment. A laxative multiplies the step, and because a step is a
whole number, the fraction is carried to next tickle rather than thrown away.
When a unit runs out of increments it leaves the tummy and lands in the
**bowel**, adding its bulk.

**Every food tickle, fluids:** a fraction of the tummy pool moves into **blood
fluid**. Blood fluid has a set-point. Below it, she's thirsty. **Only the fluid
above the set-point flows on to the bladder**, at a rate proportional to how far
above. A diuretic adds bulk straight to the bladder, from nowhere, which is on
your open list.

That one rule does three jobs: drinking cures thirst first and makes pee second,
a thirsty hero barely pees, and bleeding makes her thirstier and drier at once.

**Two ways out.**

Bowel fill against bowel capacity gives the **poop urge**. Poop stun stacks stun
her at the instant the urge starts, so her first few chances to hold are simply
missed. Past the certain-mess count she messes at least a little, guaranteed.

Bladder fill against bladder capacity gives the **holding line** and the
**desperate line**. Past desperate, a countdown runs — its length set by the
second continence effect, bladder strength. Zero means a **hold check**, then it
restarts.

**The hold check is two rolls**: strength, and confidence weighted by pride.
Both get worse the fuller she is.

- both pass → she holds
- one fails → **dribble**, and one overflow stack
- both fail → **void**, the whole thing
- fill hits capacity between checks → automatic void, no roll, and the
  **voluntary switch** and **sensation** both switch off for a while

**Three roads to incontinence**: dependence past its threshold, overflow past
its threshold, or all four effects maxed.

and I think you're right that it's owed one.
