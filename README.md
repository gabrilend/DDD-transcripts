# Double Diaper Dungeon

A roguelike auto-battler where every hero is a grown adult who is also, in her
heart, a baby. You build a guild, you send them down, and they come back
changed.

This project lives in two repositories, and you are looking at one of them.

This one holds the talking: every conversation that built the game, in order, with the dates they happened on.

The other one is the game itself, which is not open to the public yet.

## Adults only

Everybody in this game is an adult. Every single one.

But they have to be babies in their hearts, or **what is even the point.**

That is not a disclaimer and it is not a warning label. It is the game. The
regression, the diapers, the incontinence, the caregiving, the crying — that is
what the game is made of, the way a racing game is made of cars.

## Cool people only

If you do not like ABDL stuff, that is fine, go and do something else.

If you do not like ABDL *people* — flip off, dive off a diving board into the
shadow realm, and **stay there.**

Adult babies are queer people. We are a culture like any other culture, with
our own words and our own jokes and our own way of being kind to each other,
and cultures deserve games of their own. This one is ours.

## Made with a lot of AI, on purpose

There is a great deal of AI-generated work in here and that is a deliberate
choice, not an embarrassment.

AI is the great equalizer. About ninety percent of people cannot write computer
code, and that has always meant that ninety percent of people could not make
the thing they could see in their head. That wall came down. New mediums opened
up for everyone who was standing on the wrong side of it, and people who were
never going to learn a programming language can now make the thing anyway.

Good.

## Who made this

I am a software developer. I do not have money and I do not have a job, because
I am queer and I am bad at being a job.

What I do have is knowing how computers work. I know my way around a website, a
motherboard, and a command line interface. I know the right questions to ask
and the design patterns to reach for, which makes this easier for me than it
would be for most people.

It is still hard. It is still effort, and I would like the effort recognized.

I am basically a baby who can talk and who happens to have three hundred years
of life experience. Okay, thirty-two. But babies are creative, and experience
makes you experienced, and imagination plus experience is wisdom.

## Stickers

There is a fridge in this project, at `fridge.tsv`, and there are stickers on
it. Gold stars, apples, frowny faces, worms. The game reads that file and shows
every player how many the developer earned while making the patch they are
playing. Run `./sticker-report` to see the count.

Nothing is ever peeled off it. A sticker taken back gets a line saying so, and
the record of it having been given stays.
